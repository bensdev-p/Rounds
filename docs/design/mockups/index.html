Acute pancreatitis

Reviewed by Dr. J. Patel, PGY-3, on Nov 2

Internal medicineSurgery

Who gets it

Gallstones (most common) or heavy alcohol use; also hypertriglyceridemia, post-ERCP.

Key findings

Sudden epigastric pain radiating to the back, nausea, vomiting. Lipase over 3 times the upper limit of normal.

Diagnosis

Two of three: typical pain, lipase over 3× normal, imaging findings.

Management

Aggressive fluids (lactated Ringer's), pain control, early oral feeding as tolerated.
